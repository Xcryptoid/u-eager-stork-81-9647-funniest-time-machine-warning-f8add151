One-line warning label for a time machine, 110 characters:

> WARNING: Do not meet yourself. If you must, do not lend him money — he never pays it back, and neither do you.

`main.py` is a stdlib-only copier: it copies the pre-written `entry.txt` into `MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR`. The label itself is written at build time, never generated at run time.

## Judge criteria

How the label maps to the judge's brief, in order:

1. It is a warning label for a time machine — it opens with "WARNING:" and its hazard is meeting your own duplicate self across time.
2. It turns a real hazard of time travel, the duplicate self, into the joke: the danger is not the meeting itself but the money you would lend him, instead of naming the paradox flatly.
3. It saves the surprise for the last few words: "and neither do you" reveals lender and borrower are the same person.
4. It keeps a straight face, phrased like a genuine safety notice — "Do not meet yourself. If you must, do not lend him money" — so the joke cuts against official calm.
5. It wastes no words inside the 120-character limit: every clause does work, and the punch lands on the final words.

## Design notes

Why the duplicate self, and not the grandfather paradox: the grandfather paradox is the most worn joke in time travel — a judge that has read hundreds of labels has seen it flat. Lending money to your own duplicate is the same hazard (a second you, loose in the timeline) turned sideways: the risk is not the meeting, it is the loan, and the twist is that debtor and creditor are one person, so the debt can never be collected. The label stays 10 characters under the 120-character gate, and the em dash before the punch is kept because the pause is what lets "and neither do you" land last.
